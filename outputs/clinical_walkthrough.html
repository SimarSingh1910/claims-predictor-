4 · How severity of a single value is graded

The question clinicians ask most: what does the engine actually do with an HbA1c of 8?

It is neither rejected nor capped. A value of 8% is a genuine measurement of poor glycaemic control. The engine represents it in five ways at once, because no single representation captures everything clinically relevant:

RepresentationValueWhat it encodes
Raw measurement8.0the magnitude itself
Diabetic-range flag1crosses the 6.5% diagnostic threshold
Pre-diabetic flag0past that band, into frank diabetes
Abnormality count+1one of eight abnormal findings met
Estimated average glucose182.928.7 × 8.0 − 46.7 mg/dL

Severity is counted, not measured

Each rule asks a yes/no question — above or below this cut-point? — never how far above? Severity is captured because different rules use different cut-points, and a patient clears as many as their value permits:

rule 12HbA1c above 6.45 %crossed
rule 87HbA1c above 7.20 %crossed
rule 203HbA1c above 9.10 %not crossed
rule 341HbA1c above 11.5 %not crossed
severity contribution  ∝  number of cut-points crossed

The honest clinical consequence. Severity is graded in steps rather than continuously. Two patients at 8.0% and 8.9% are treated identically, because no rule separates them — a genuine loss of granularity. The compensating benefit is robustness: a transcription error of 80 instead of 8.0 lands on exactly the same rungs and cannot distort the result, which matters when data arrives from many laboratories.

6 · Questions a clinician usually asks

What if the HbA1c were 12 rather than 8?

Very little would change. Each rule asks which side of a cut-point the value falls, not how far past. A value of 12 would clear one or two additional rungs and score somewhat higher — but 8.0% and 8.9% are treated identically. Severity is graded in steps.

What if a lab value is missing?

If it is one of the six essential values, the engine refuses to score and reports the patient as unscored with the reason. It does not substitute a population average — an invented creatinine produces a confident number about kidney function nobody measured.

Is this applied to individual patients?

No. Individual probabilities are averaged into age-and-sex cohorts, and the cohort figure prices the group policy. No clinical decision is taken about any individual.

Where do the cut-points come from?

Two sources. The eight abnormal-range flags use established clinical thresholds — HbA1c 6.5, BP 140/90, eGFR 60, BMI 30. The cut-points inside the 400 rules are learned from the training data, which is why they land at values like 6.45 or 7.20 rather than round numbers.

Can it explain an individual prediction?

Partially. The engine can report which parameters contributed most across the workforce, and which flags a given patient raised. It cannot produce a clean per-patient causal account, because the score is spread across 400 overlapping rules.

7 · Does it work? — the honest answer

The headline result On real member data, once a confounder was properly removed, the engine performed no better than chance at predicting hospitalisation. The confidence interval brackets random guessing.

What the confounder was

Early results looked considerably better, and the reason turned out to be a coverage artefact rather than a clinical signal. Covered parents appear in the dataset almost exclusively when hospitalised — they rarely attend routine check-ups. The engine therefore learned, quite correctly for this dataset, that "is a covered parent" predicts hospitalisation. That is a statement about who attends screening, not about physiology.

Removing it required excluding those patients entirely, not merely removing the relationship variable — age alone reconstructs "is a parent" with an AUC of 0.88. Once dependants were excluded by row, performance fell to chance.

Why this is clinically unsurprising

The cohort is a young corporate workforce, mean age approximately 33. Hospitalisation in such a population is dominated by:

CauseForeseeable from an annual blood panel?
Obstetric — 27% of admissions were maternity-relatedNo — a planned event, unrelated to metabolic markers
Trauma and accidentsNo
Acute infectionNo
Chronic disease decompensationIn principle yes — but uncommon at this age

Annual metabolic screening is designed to detect chronic disease trajectory over years, not acute admission within twelve months. The absence of signal is a mismatch between instrument and endpoint, not a failure of the instrument.

A data-quality issue worth knowing HbA1c is unreliable in our records for a non-statistical reason: 168 of 576 rows contain exactly 5.4%, against only 38 distinct values in the whole column. That is a default written in where the assay was not performed. Roughly 30% of the column is a placeholder — which is why HbA1c behaved counter-clinically in every model version, appearing to reduce risk as it rose. The engine's handling is correct; the input is not.

What it isA population risk calculator, structurally similar to QRISK, with hospitalisation claim as the endpoint
InputsStandard AHC panel — demographics, vitals, glycaemic, lipid, renal, hepatic, haematology, thyroid
OutputA calibrated probability per person, aggregated to a workforce for premium setting
Abnormal valuesKept as measured; graded by how many clinical cut-points they cross
ComorbidityRepresented as conjunctions, so combinations can exceed the sum of their parts

ValidationAt chance once the coverage confounder was removed; reported as a null
Clinical useNone. No individual clinical decision rests on this output
Where this leaves the work

The pipeline — ingestion, clinical feature derivation, calibration, and evaluation against pre-registered stopping rules — is complete and reproducible. What it has established is a well-characterised negative result: annual check-up biochemistry does not predict twelve-month hospitalisation in this workforce, and the apparent signal in earlier analyses was a screening-attendance artefact.

The next component is cost given a claim, where the drivers are demographic and diagnostic rather than biochemical, and where the data does carry genuine signal.
